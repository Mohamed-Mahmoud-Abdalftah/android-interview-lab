# BG-01

## Original interview question

When should you use a coroutine, WorkManager, foreground service, or AlarmManager?

## What the interviewer evaluates

Select lifecycle coroutine, persistent work, foreground service or exact alarm by lifetime and user visibility.

## Explanation in Arabic

اختيار background API يبدأ بالسؤال: الشغل مرتبط بالشاشة؟ `viewModelScope`/lifecycle coroutine تكفي. لازم ينجو من خروج التطبيق أو reboot؟ WorkManager مع state محفوظة. شغل طويل المستخدم شايفه وقد يحتاج تشغيل مستمر؟ foreground service وفق type/notification/restrictions الحالية. موعد دقيق لتذكير فعلي؟ AlarmManager exact إذا الأهلية والإذن يسمحوا. ولا API تضمن second-perfect periodic execution.

## Practical walkthrough

لو طلب إرسال صورة يختفي مع الشاشة، coroutine في `viewModelScope` لا تضمن استكماله بعد process death. لو المستخدم محتاج upload مؤجل مع retry، خزّن job في DB وشغّل WorkManager. لو تسجيل صوت مباشر مرئي، foreground service بالنوع والإشعار والقيود المناسبة. Exact alarm فقط لو فعل user-facing يتطلب توقيتًا دقيقًا وتسمح به السياسة. لا تخلط “يعمل في الخلفية” مع “يعمل في ثانية محددة”؛ Android scheduler قد يؤخر العمل.

## Why it matters and how it works

Coroutines give structured in-process work. WorkManager persists scheduling with constraints and may delay execution. Foreground services require an ongoing user-visible notification and are restricted when starting from background. Exact alarms are for genuinely time-critical alarm/calendar use cases, not routine sync.

## Trade-offs and decision criteria

WorkManager improves durability but trades away exact timing. Foreground service offers ongoing execution subject to platform rules and notification cost. Exact alarm may require special access and harms battery. Choose the narrowest mechanism that satisfies user need.

## Failure modes

Putting upload only in viewModelScope loses it on navigation/process death. Scheduling ordinary polling with exact alarms fights power policy and may be disallowed. Starting foreground service from background can throw on newer Android versions.

## Production perspective

A media app persists an upload and schedules WorkManager; UI observes progress. A user-started live navigation session may use an eligible foreground service. A calendar alarm uses exact alarm only when precise delivery is core to the feature.

## Interview answer — short

“I choose by lifetime and timing: lifecycle coroutine for screen work, WorkManager for restartable durable work, foreground service for eligible ongoing visible work, and exact alarm for genuine precise reminders. None gives arbitrary second-perfect periodic execution.”

## Interview answer — structured

“The first question is whether the operation may be cancelled when the screen goes away. If yes, a lifecycle-owned coroutine is simplest. If it must complete after app exit or reboot, I persist the operation and use WorkManager under constraints, accepting flexible timing. If the user actively expects ongoing, visible work, I consider a foreground service and its type, notification and background-start restrictions. If a real alarm must fire at a precise time, AlarmManager may be appropriate, subject to permissions and Doze policies.”

## Interview answer — deep senior

“Background execution is a product and OS contract. A coroutine cannot survive process death; WorkManager can reschedule persisted requests, but its worker may run more than once and not at an exact second. A foreground service is for ongoing user-visible work and must meet current Android type/permission/notification rules; starting one while backgrounded has restrictions. Exact alarms are exceptional, permission-sensitive and not a substitute for periodic sync. I persist business intent before scheduling, make remote writes idempotent, expose status to UI and test force-stop/reboot/network transitions on supported devices. I never promise a timing guarantee the platform does not provide. Coroutines give structured in-process work. For example, a search request tied to a visible screen can be canceled with its ViewModel; a photo upload requested by the user needs a durable file reference and WorkManager if it must survive closure; live navigation may need an eligible foreground service; a calendar reminder may qualify for an exact alarm. I would ask what happens when the phone is offline or powered off, because none of these APIs can guarantee a remote financial action in that state. The UI should show pending work and the backend should own deadline-sensitive obligations. I verify the chosen path on current target SDKs, especially permissions and notification behavior.”

## How to answer in the interview

- **Why:** ownership and timing determine API.
- **How:** classify lifetime, deferrability and user visibility.
- **Trade-off:** durability versus immediacy.
- **Failure case:** ViewModel coroutine treated as persistent upload.
- **Production solution:** persist intent and choose compliant scheduler.
