# BG-17

## Original interview question

A business requirement asks for second-perfect periodic background execution. What Android limitations matter?

## What the interviewer evaluates

Reject second-perfect periodic execution promises under Android power/background limits.

## Concept and technical explanation

Addressing a business requirement demanding second-perfect periodic background execution on modern Android requires establishing that the operating system intentionally and fundamentally prohibits such execution. Android's core architectural evolution—from Doze mode and App Standby Buckets in API 23 to background execution limits in API 26, AlarmManager throttling in API 31, and foreground service restrictions in API 34—is explicitly engineered to eradicate periodic background wakeups that deplete battery life, consume cellular radios, and compromise thermal stability. WorkManager enforces a platform minimum periodic interval of 15 minutes (PeriodicWorkRequest.MIN_PERIODIC_INTERVAL_MILLIS) with indeterminate flex windows. AlarmManager repeating alarms were converted into inexact alarms in API 19, and exact alarms are aggressively throttled during Doze sleep cycles. Furthermore, background services are terminated within minutes, and foreground dataSync services face hard 6-hour timeouts in Android 14.

```kotlin
    am.setExactAndAllowWhileIdle(AlarmManager.RTC_WAKEUP, targetEpochMs, pendingIntent)

// Backend determines timing and wakes the device via high-priority data message

        // App is granted a brief high-priority window to process data and update UI/notify user
    }
}
```

## Why it matters and how it works

If exact per-second computation is a business rule, run it on a server or dedicated controlled hardware, not an arbitrary consumer Android process. For user-visible live sessions, an eligible foreground service may run while the session is active but still cannot promise perfect timing through power loss. Persist and reconcile timestamps/events after reconnect.

## Trade-offs and decision criteria

Server-side scheduling improves timing under controlled infrastructure but adds connectivity and cost. A foreground session can improve live UX with notification/power use. Deferrable local sync via WorkManager is battery-friendly but has flexible timing. Explain these tradeoffs to product before committing an SLA.

## Failure modes

Building a loop with delay(1000) in an application scope dies with process. Repeated exact alarms may be restricted and drain battery. A Worker configured every 15 minutes cannot satisfy per-second behavior. Hidden FGS can violate platform policy and user trust.

## Production perspective

A logistics app lets backend own per-second dispatch deadlines. Android client displays last-known status, listens for push while possible, and catches up by server cursor. An active driver navigation session may use eligible foreground execution with notification, but backend remains authoritative.

## Interview answer — short

Android strictly prohibits second-perfect periodic background execution to protect battery life and thermal stability. WorkManager enforces a hard 15-minute minimum interval and flex windows. AlarmManager repeating alarms are inexact, and exact alarms are throttled in Doze mode to once every 9 to 15 minutes. Background services are killed, and foreground services face strict type limits and execution caps in Android 14.

## Interview answer — structured

When business stakeholders request second-perfect periodic background execution, a senior engineer must educate them on Android platform realities and constraints.
The Android operating system is architected to eliminate background timers to preserve battery life and prevent thermal throttling.
WorkManager enforces a hard platform floor of 15 minutes for PeriodicWorkRequest; specifying intervals below 15 minutes is automatically clamped to 15 minutes, with additional delays imposed by Doze mode maintenance windows and App Standby Buckets.
AlarmManager cannot deliver periodic second-level precision either. Platform updates made repeating alarms inexact. While developers can reschedule individual exact alarms using setExactAndAllowWhileIdle, the platform limits idle wakeups to once every 9 to 15 minutes when the device enters Doze mode. Attempting to misuse exact alarms for periodic polling risks immediate rejection under Google Play policies.
Finally, Foreground Services cannot run indefinitely for general polling: Android 14 limits dataSync services to 6 hours within a 24-hour window.
The proper engineering resolution is pushing back on the requirement, proposing server-driven FCM push notifications, or capturing data in foreground sessions.

## Interview answer — deep senior

Under the hood, Android power management is governed by Linux kernel low-power states, hardware wake locks, and system services including PowerManagerService and BatteryStatsService. When the device screen turns off and remains undisturbed, the platform transitions into Deep Doze. In this state, the application processor enters deep sleep (suspend-to-RAM), clock frequencies drop, the cellular modem enters low-power discontinuous reception (DRX) states, and iptables firewall rules block application socket connections.
Attempting to maintain a second-perfect periodic loop in the background fundamentally conflicts with this hardware and OS architecture. If an application attempts to hold a continuous Partial WakeLock to keep CPU cores active, modern Android releases detect excessive wake lock holding and flag the app via Android Vitals for bad behavior, penalizing its Google Play store visibility.
Furthermore, Android 14 introduced strict foreground service timeout policies, where any dataSync service running longer than 6 hours is forcibly killed.
Senior engineers address this business requirement by re-framing the problem. If the use case involves real-time telemetry, the application should maintain a persistent WebSocket connection only while the app is in the foreground, dropping to server-sent high-priority FCM notifications when backgrounded.
If the requirement involves medical or critical tracking, the app must be registered as a specialized foreground service (e.g., location or connectedDevice) with explicit hardware device pairing. For all standard use cases, the engineer negotiates an acceptable deferrable threshold aligned with WorkManager's 15-minute batched cadence.

## How to answer in the interview

- **Why:** OS cannot promise arbitrary second-perfect work.
- **How:** translate SLA to supported execution mode.
- **Trade-off:** freshness versus battery and permission.
- **Failure case:** promise exact periodic polling with WorkManager.
- **Production solution:** server schedule or qualified visible session.

## Follow-up questions

- **Interviewer:** "Can `AlarmManager.setRepeating()` be used for a 1-second interval?"
  **Answer:** "No. Since Android 4.4, AlarmManager automatically inexacts and batches repeating alarms to much longer intervals; 1-second repeat is impossible."

- **Interviewer:** "What is the battery impact of holding a `WakeLock` continuously?"
  **Answer:** "A continuous partial WakeLock keeps the CPU active at high power states, draining a full battery in 3–5 hours even with the screen off."
