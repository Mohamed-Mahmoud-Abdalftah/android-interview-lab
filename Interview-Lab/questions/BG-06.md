# BG-06

## Original interview question

What are Doze mode and app standby restrictions?

## What the interviewer evaluates

Explain Doze and app standby as OS power controls affecting background timing.

## Explanation in Arabic

WorkManager يحترم scheduling policy وقد يتأخر؛ لا تعد المستخدم بمزامنة عند 02:00 بالضبط. للـUI اعرض pending state وlast sync time، ولا تخفي تأخير الشبكة. اختبر سيناريوهات idle وrestricted battery على أجهزة مدعومة، واعتمد backend alerts للالتزامات الحرجة بدل اعتبار الهاتف متصل دائمًا.

## Why it matters and how it works

Power policy varies by Android version, device state and app usage bucket. Do not infer that plugged-in or foreground status always gives identical behavior on every OEM. Test with official adb/device tools and observe actual last-success rather than scheduled time.

## Trade-offs and decision criteria

Respecting Doze saves battery and system health but increases background latency. Expedited work and foreground services have quotas/restrictions and must match user-visible urgency. Server push may notify, but offline/reconnect catch-up still required.

## Failure modes

A design that promises sync every minute while the device sleeps will fail under power policy. Repeated wake locks or exact alarms for routine polling abuse battery and may be restricted. Treat missed scheduled windows as normal, not data corruption.

## Production perspective

A field app records edits locally and shows Pending while device is idle; WorkManager syncs when permitted and on next app foreground. Metrics track pending age and server acknowledgement so product knows when delay exceeds expectations.

## Interview answer — short

“Doze and app standby defer background network and jobs to save power. WorkManager respects these restrictions, so I design deferrable, durable sync and show freshness rather than promise exact periodic timing.”

## Interview answer — structured

“Android may delay background execution when a device is idle or an app is rarely used. Doze and app standby affect jobs, network and alarms, so a periodic WorkManager schedule is not a minute-accurate timer. I persist operations before scheduling, let WorkManager run under constraints, and refresh on foreground or reconnect. The UI exposes pending/stale status. A genuinely precise user-facing reminder may use the platform’s exact-alarm path, subject to eligibility and permission.”

## Interview answer — deep senior

“I design for power management as normal operating conditions. A worker can be deferred, batched or retried according to Doze, standby bucket and constraints; the app cannot promise a strict wall-clock sync interval. I keep an outbox and last-success timestamp, make workers idempotent and reconcile after long offline periods. If urgency is truly user-visible, I evaluate expedited work or an eligible foreground service under current quotas/restrictions; exact alarm is reserved for a real alarm use case. Tests use official device/adb idle modes and varied Android versions, then compare actual execution timestamps with product SLA. I do not use wake locks or alarm spam as a shortcut for ordinary periodic polling. A device in Doze may batch network and jobs, and standby treatment depends on recent app use and system policy. Therefore I show a pending state and last successful sync time rather than a fake “synced” badge when the Worker was merely enqueued. For an obligation with a strict business deadline, I move the deadline and escalation to the backend; the client can cooperate but may be offline, restricted or powered down. I test under idle/restricted modes on supported devices and capture how long work waits, without equating one emulator run to every OEM. If users need immediate action while actively using the app, I use a user-initiated path that meets platform rules.”
